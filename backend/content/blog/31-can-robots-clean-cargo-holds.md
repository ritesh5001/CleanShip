Every few months a video does the rounds of a robot crawling up a ship's side, or a remotely operated cannon blasting a cargo hold clean while the crew watches from deck. It's natural to ask: are we close to robots replacing people in hold cleaning?

Short answer: not yet, but parts of the job are being automated, and some of it is genuinely useful.

## What kinds of hold cleaning automation exist?

**Fixed or semi-fixed washing systems.** Some bulk carriers have high-volume washing nozzles or cannons fitted in the hold or on the coaming, operated from deck. They're effectively automated combi-guns: good for rough washing and rinsing large areas, without people in the hold.

**Remote-controlled washing cannons on masts or stands.** Portable units placed in the hold and aimed remotely. They reduce time spent by people in the hold during the dirtiest stages.

**Magnetic crawler robots.** Robots that cling to steel using magnets and drive across vertical and overhead surfaces. They're well established for hull and tank inspection (thickness measurement, visual inspection) and in some industrial settings for blasting and cleaning. In cargo holds, they're used mainly for inspection and are being developed for cleaning.

**Drones.** Used increasingly for close visual inspection of hold structure, especially upper frames, without access equipment. They don't clean, but they make inspection of the upper structure much quicker.

## Can magnetic crawler robots clean vertical hold structures?

On flat or gently curved plating, they can carry HP nozzles or blasting heads and clean or prepare surfaces. The challenge in bulk carrier holds is geometry: side frames, brackets, stiffeners and corrugations create a maze of edges and corners that a crawler struggles to traverse. Residue sits exactly where crawlers can't reach, on the top faces of brackets and behind frames.

## Can robots replace manual hold cleaning?

Not today. Robots and remote systems can help with:

- **Rough washing** of large, open surfaces
- **Rinsing**
- **Inspection** of the upper structure (drones, crawlers)
- **Reducing time people spend in the hold** during the dirtiest stages

They can't yet reliably handle:

- **Bilge wells:** confined, cluttered, full of sludge
- **Detail cleaning:** behind frames, on top of brackets, ladders, pipe guards
- **Judgement:** deciding whether a mark is residue or discolouration, whether rust is loose or tight
- **Chemical application** with the right dwell time on the right areas
- **Rust removal and coating repair**
- **Pre-inspection** to the standard a surveyor applies

## Where automation makes sense now

- Ships on repetitive trades with dirty cargoes, where fixed washing systems pay back quickly
- Inspection of the upper structure with drones, especially for planned maintenance
- Reducing exposure of people to the worst stages of washing

## Where people are still essential

Anywhere detail, judgement and access matter, which is most of what decides whether a hold passes. A surveyor inspects with a torch and a scraper, looking at the places hardest to reach. Getting those places clean still needs people with HP lances at arm's length, whether on a cherry picker, staging or [rope access](/blog/cleaning-cargo-hold-upper-frames-without-scaffolding).

## Will it change?

Probably, gradually. As crawlers get better at navigating complex geometry and as ship designs evolve, more of the washing will be automated. But for the foreseeable future, the job will be a mix: automation for the rough work and inspection, people for the detail, judgement and final standard.

For now, the fastest way to get holds to grain clean remains a well-equipped team with the right chemicals, the right access and enough people. See [how to clean a bulk carrier cargo hold](/blog/how-to-clean-a-bulk-carrier-cargo-hold).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can robots clean bulk carrier cargo holds?
Partly. Remote-controlled cannons and fixed washing systems can rough-wash and rinse large surfaces, but robots can't yet reliably handle bilge wells, detail cleaning behind frames or the judgement needed for inspection.

### Can magnetic crawler robots clean vertical hold structures?
On flat plating they can carry cleaning heads, but frames, brackets and stiffeners make bulk carrier holds difficult for crawlers, and residue sits where they can't reach.

### Can robots replace manual hold cleaning?
Not yet. Automation helps with rough washing, rinsing and inspection, but people are still needed for detail cleaning, bilges, chemicals, rust removal and final pre-inspection.

### Are drones used for cargo hold inspection?
Yes. Drones are increasingly used for close visual inspection of the upper hold structure without access equipment, though they don't clean.

### What hold cleaning tasks can be automated today?
Rough washing and rinsing of large open surfaces with fixed or remote-controlled washing systems, and visual inspection with drones and crawlers.
