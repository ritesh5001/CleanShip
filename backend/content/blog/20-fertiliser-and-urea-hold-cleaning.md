Fertiliser looks like one of the easier cargoes to clean. Most of it dissolves in water, so you'd think a good wash would do. And it nearly does. The trouble is in the "nearly": the thin film of salts left behind, and what it does to a hold over the following days.

## What makes fertiliser residue tricky

Bulk fertilisers include urea, ammonium nitrate and ammonium nitrate-based blends, ammonium sulphate, potash (potassium chloride), phosphates such as DAP and MAP, and many compound blends. They behave differently, but most share a few properties:

- **They're hygroscopic.** They pull moisture from the air. A film of fertiliser residue on a "dry" hold will turn damp and sticky.
- **Many are corrosive.** Salts like potassium chloride and ammonium compounds attack steel, especially when damp.
- **They cake and stick.** Damp fertiliser sets into a hard crust on ledges and in corners.
- **Some have strong odours.** Urea and ammonium products can leave an ammonia smell.
- **Some are hazardous.** Ammonium nitrate is an oxidiser, and some fertilisers are covered by specific IMSBC Code entries with their own requirements. Check the schedule for the cargo you carried.

## Why is fresh water washing important after fertiliser?

Seawater washing alone leaves two salts behind: the sea's and the fertiliser's. Both are hygroscopic and corrosive. Fresh water dissolves and removes fertiliser salts and chlorides, leaving a hold that will actually stay dry.

Many fertilisers are highly soluble, so fresh water does most of the cleaning work. A thorough fresh water wash is often more effective than chemicals for fertiliser residue.

## How do you clean fertiliser residue from cargo holds?

1. **Remove as much as possible during discharge.** Ask stevedores to clear ledges and corners.
2. **Sweep thoroughly.** Fertiliser that has caked in corners may need scraping.
3. **Clean hatch covers, coamings and drain channels.** Fertiliser dust gets everywhere.
4. **Clear the bilge wells.** Fertiliser residue can form a hard crust in wells and strainers.
5. **Wash with seawater, top down,** to remove the bulk of the dissolved residue.
6. **Fresh water wash, thoroughly, top down.** Don't skimp. This is the stage that matters.
7. **Dry quickly.** Ventilate, mop and pump bilges dry. A fertiliser hold that stays damp starts to corrode.
8. **Inspect for crusts, stains and odour.**

## How do you clean urea from a bulk carrier hold?

Urea is very soluble, so it washes out easily. The challenges are:

- **Caking:** damp urea sets hard in corners and on ledges. Scrape and remove before washing.
- **Odour:** urea and its breakdown products can smell of ammonia. Thorough fresh water washing and ventilation usually clear it.
- **Moisture:** urea residue pulls moisture from the air, so holds need a thorough final fresh water rinse and complete drying.

## How do you remove fertiliser stains?

Most fertilisers wash out completely with fresh water. Stains that remain are usually:

- **Rust bloom** from corrosion while the residue sat damp. Treat as rust: remove loose material, rinse and dry.
- **Discolouration of the coating,** from long exposure. May be accepted if it doesn't transfer. Test with a white cloth.
- **Residue from phosphate or compound fertilisers** that's less soluble. A suitable cleaning chemical followed by HP washing usually removes it.

## Fertiliser to grain cleaning procedure

Grain surveyors look closely for crystalline residue, odour and moisture after fertiliser, so:

1. Dry removal of caked residue
2. Clear bilge wells
3. Seawater wash, top down
4. Inspect for remaining crusts; scrape and treat as needed
5. Thorough fresh water wash of every surface
6. Inspect for odour, residue and stains; repeat locally
7. Final fresh water rinse
8. Dry and ventilate thoroughly
9. Bilge wells cleaned, tested and prepared
10. Pre-inspect to grain standard

See [how to prepare cargo holds for grain](/blog/how-to-prepare-cargo-holds-for-grain) for the full checklist.

## Preparing holds before loading fertiliser

Fertiliser is commonly loaded to grain clean or higher, because receivers don't want contamination, and some fertilisers react badly with residues of other cargoes. Britannia's guidance lists fertiliser among cargoes that may be associated with both grain clean and hospital clean standards. Check the charterer's instructions. Holds must be dry, because moisture causes the cargo to cake.

## Safety

- Check the IMSBC Code entry for the specific fertiliser, especially for ammonium nitrate-based products
- Ventilate before entry and test the atmosphere. Some fertilisers can release ammonia
- Wear eye protection, gloves and respiratory protection against dust
- Keep fertiliser residue away from oils and combustibles

## Frequently asked questions

### How do you clean fertiliser residue from a cargo hold?
Remove caked residue, sweep, clear bilge wells, wash with seawater and then thoroughly with fresh water, top down, and dry the hold quickly.

### Why is fresh water washing important after fertiliser?
Fertiliser residue and seawater salts are both hygroscopic and corrosive. Fresh water dissolves and removes them, so the hold stays dry and corrosion is prevented.

### How do you clean urea from a bulk carrier hold?
Scrape out caked urea, then wash thoroughly. Urea is very soluble, so fresh water washing removes it well. Ventilate to clear any ammonia odour, and dry completely.

### How do you remove fertiliser stains in cargo holds?
Most fertiliser washes out with fresh water. Remaining marks are usually rust bloom or coating discolouration. Less soluble residues can be removed with a suitable chemical and HP washing.

### What standard is needed to load fertiliser?
Often grain clean or higher, depending on the charterer's and receiver's requirements. Holds must be clean and dry to prevent contamination and caking.
