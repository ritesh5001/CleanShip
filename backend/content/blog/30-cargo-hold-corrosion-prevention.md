Corrosion in cargo holds isn't just a cleaning nuisance. It's steel wastage that class surveyors measure, that can lead to expensive renewals, and in the worst cases to structural failure. Most of it is caused by things that happen between cargoes, and much of it can be slowed down with habits that cost very little.

## What causes corrosion in cargo holds?

- **Water and oxygen.** The basics. Wet steel corrodes; dry steel doesn't, or barely.
- **Salt (chlorides).** From seawater washing and salt cargoes. Salt keeps steel damp and accelerates corrosion. See [salt cargo hold cleaning](/blog/salt-cargo-hold-cleaning).
- **Acidic residues.** Sulphur forms acid when wet. Some coals contain sulphur that can form acidic run-off. See [sulphur hold cleaning](/blog/sulphur-hold-cleaning-and-limewashing).
- **Hygroscopic cargoes.** Fertilisers and salt pull moisture from the air and hold it against the steel.
- **Mechanical damage.** Grabs, bulldozers and abrasive cargoes strip coating, exposing bare steel.
- **Condensation (ship sweat).** Temperature differences between cargo, air and steel cause condensation on the steel.
- **Neglected coating.** Small breakdowns become large areas of corrosion if not repaired.

## How do you prevent corrosion in cargo holds?

**1. Keep the coating sound.** Coating is the first line of defence. Inspect after every cargo and repair breakdown promptly. See [hold painting and coating repair](/blog/cargo-hold-painting-and-coating-repair).

**2. Finish every wash with fresh water.** Removing chlorides is one of the cheapest anti-corrosion measures. Britannia's guidance stresses having enough fresh water for a final thorough rinse of all holds.

**3. Dry the holds.** Ventilate, open hatches where safe, mop up standing water and pump the bilges dry.

**4. Clean corrosive residues promptly.** Sulphur, salt and fertiliser should be washed out as soon as possible after discharge.

**5. Use barrier coatings for corrosive cargoes.** Limewash or proprietary barrier coatings protect steel from sulphur, salt and dirty cargoes such as green and calcined petcoke and coal, as Britannia notes.

**6. Reduce mechanical damage.** Ask stevedores to take care with grabs and bulldozers on the tank top and hoppers. Record damage and report it.

**7. Ventilate to control sweat.** Proper cargo ventilation reduces condensation on the steel during the voyage.

## How do you treat corrosion once it's there?

1. **Assess it.** Is it surface rust, scale, pitting or wastage? Measure thickness if wastage is suspected. Class may need to be involved.
2. **Remove loose rust and scale.** See [removing rust and loose paint](/blog/remove-rust-and-loose-paint-from-cargo-holds).
3. **Clean and remove salts.**
4. **Prepare the surface** to the coating manufacturer's standard.
5. **Apply a compatible coating system.**
6. **Monitor** the area after subsequent cargoes.

Pitting and wastage beyond limits are structural issues that need repair, not just painting. Follow the class society's guidance.

## How do you prevent corrosion after sulphur cargo?

- Limewash or barrier coat before loading
- Wash out with fresh water promptly after discharge, as the IMSBC Code requires
- Dry thoroughly
- Inspect and repair coating after each sulphur voyage

## Corrosion after salt and fertiliser

- Wash out promptly with fresh water
- Dry thoroughly
- Consider barrier coatings for regular trades

## The role of inspection

You can't prevent corrosion you can't see. Regular hold inspection, after every discharge and cleaning, catches coating breakdown and corrosion early. Hold residue hides damage, which is why load-on-top voyages over an extended period, with no proper inspection in between, can hide developing problems.

For upper structure inspection, close-up access via cherry picker, staging or [rope access](/services/hold-cleaning/rope-access) is often needed.

## Frequently asked questions

### What causes corrosion in cargo holds?
Water and oxygen, chlorides from seawater and salt cargoes, acidic residues such as sulphur, hygroscopic cargoes like fertiliser, mechanical coating damage, condensation and neglected coating.

### How do you prevent corrosion in cargo holds?
Keep the coating sound, finish every wash with fresh water, dry holds thoroughly, wash out corrosive residues promptly, use barrier coatings for corrosive cargoes, reduce mechanical damage and ventilate to control sweat.

### How do you treat corrosion in a cargo hold?
Assess the extent, remove loose rust and scale, clean and de-salt, prepare the surface and apply a compatible coating. Pitting or wastage beyond limits needs structural repair.

### How do you prevent corrosion after a sulphur cargo?
Limewash or barrier coat before loading, wash out with fresh water promptly after discharge, dry thoroughly and repair coating damage.

### Why does a fresh water rinse help prevent corrosion?
It removes chlorides left by seawater washing and salt cargoes, which otherwise keep steel damp and accelerate corrosion.
