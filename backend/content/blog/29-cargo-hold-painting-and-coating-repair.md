Good coating makes every hold-cleaning job easier. Residue doesn't bond as stubbornly to sound paint, rust doesn't form, and hospital clean becomes possible at all. Bad coating does the opposite: every cargo bites into it, every wash lifts a little more, and every grain inspection turns into a scaling job.

Painting holds isn't a cleaning task, but it's the thing that decides how hard the next fifty cleaning jobs will be.

## When should you paint, and when should you touch up?

- **Full re-coating** of holds is a major job, usually done at drydock or a planned repair period, with blasting to a high surface standard and a full multi-coat system.
- **Touch-up and spot repair** is done between cargoes, at anchorage or on passage, to fix local coating breakdown before it spreads.

Most ships live on touch-up between drydocks. Done regularly, it keeps the holds in good order. Neglected, it leads to large areas of breakdown that can't be fixed between cargoes.

## How do you prepare a hold for painting?

Surface preparation decides whether paint lasts weeks or years. In order:

1. **Clean.** Remove all cargo residue, oil and grease. Degreasers are often needed after petcoke or coal.
2. **Remove salts.** Wash with fresh water. Salts left under paint draw moisture through the coating and cause blistering. Many specifications set a maximum allowable salt level, tested on the surface.
3. **Remove loose rust, scale and failed coating.** Hand tools, power tools, or UHP water jetting. See [removing rust and loose paint](/blog/remove-rust-and-loose-paint-from-cargo-holds).
4. **Achieve the specified surface standard.** The coating manufacturer specifies the minimum preparation standard (for example a power-tool or blast-cleaning standard) for the product.
5. **Feather the edges** of sound coating around repair areas so the new coat blends.
6. **Dry the surface completely.** Check steel temperature is above the dew point by the margin the manufacturer requires (commonly 3°C).
7. **Paint promptly** before flash rust forms.

## How do you repair damaged hold coating?

For spot repairs:

1. Clean and remove salts from the damaged area and surrounding coating
2. Remove loose rust and coating back to sound edges
3. Prepare the steel to the standard the product requires
4. Feather the edges
5. Apply primer or the first coat, by brush or roller for small areas, by spray for larger ones
6. Apply further coats as the system requires, respecting overcoat times
7. Allow full cure before loading cargo

Use a coating compatible with the existing system and suitable for the cargoes you carry. Some cargoes, such as grain and other food products, may require coatings certified for food contact.

## How do you paint upper hold structures?

The upper structure is the hardest place to paint, for the same reason it's the hardest place to clean: access. Options:

- **Cherry picker:** good reach where one can be landed and used
- **Staging:** stable but slow to erect and strike
- **Rope access:** technicians descend from the coaming to prepare and paint at arm's length

## Can rope-access crew paint cargo holds?

Yes. Rope access technicians can carry out surface preparation (scraping, wire brushing, power tool cleaning) and coating application (brush, roller, and in some cases spray) on the upper structure without staging. ## Should tank tops be painted after rust removal?

Ideally, yes. Bare steel on a tank top rusts quickly with the next wet cargo or sweat, and the next round of scaling is harder. Tank tops take heavy mechanical damage from grabs and bulldozers, so coatings there have a hard life, but even a primer coat slows corrosion. Check that the coating is fully cured before loading.

## Painting at sea or at anchorage

Painting between cargoes is constrained by:

- **Humidity and dew point:** paint won't cure properly on damp steel
- **Temperature:** many coatings have minimum cure temperatures
- **Cure time before loading:** cargo on uncured paint means damaged coating and contaminated cargo
- **Ventilation:** solvent vapour in an enclosed hold is a health and fire hazard. Ventilate, test the atmosphere, and control ignition sources

Plan painting early in the ballast passage so the coating has time to cure.

## How often should cargo holds be maintained?

Inspect after every discharge and cleaning, as Britannia's guidance recommends, and record defects for repair as part of the ship's planned maintenance system. Touch up coating breakdown promptly. Small areas are quick to fix; large areas aren't.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do you prepare a cargo hold for painting?
Clean off residue and grease, wash with fresh water to remove salts, remove loose rust and failed coating, prepare the steel to the manufacturer's standard, feather the edges, dry the surface and paint promptly.

### How do you repair damaged hold coating?
Clean and de-salt the area, remove loose material back to sound coating, prepare the steel, feather the edges, apply a compatible primer and topcoats, and allow full cure before loading.

### Can rope access technicians paint cargo holds?
Yes. Rope access technicians can prepare and paint the upper hold structure without staging, often combining cleaning, inspection and coating in one descent.

### Should tank tops be painted after rust removal?
Ideally yes. Bare steel rusts quickly, so even a primer coat helps protect the tank top and reduces future scaling.

### How often should cargo holds be maintained?
Inspect after every discharge and cleaning, record defects, and repair coating breakdown promptly as part of the planned maintenance system.
