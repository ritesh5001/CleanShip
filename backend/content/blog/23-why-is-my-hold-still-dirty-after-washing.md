You've swept it. You've washed it twice. The HP pump has been running for ten hours. And the hold still looks grey, still has black marks on the tank top, and the Chief Officer just came back with a dusty glove from a bracket at the top of frame 60. What's going on?

Almost always it's one of a handful of problems, and each one has a fix that isn't "wash it again, harder."

## "Why does my hold still have black stains after pressure washing?"

Because the stain isn't dirt sitting on the surface. It's residue that's bonded to or into the coating: petcoke film, coal fines worked into scratches, oily residue. Water at any pressure struggles to break that bond.

**Fix:** use a chemical matched to the residue (an alkaline degreaser for coal and petcoke), apply it to a surface it can work on, give it the full dwell time, then HP wash. Then check with a white cloth: if the mark doesn't transfer, it may be paint discolouration, not residue. Read our [hold cleaning chemicals guide](/blog/hold-cleaning-chemicals) for how to do this properly.

## "Why doesn't high-pressure washing remove petcoke?"

Green petcoke leaves an oily hydrocarbon film. Oil and water don't mix, so high pressure removes the loose petcoke above the film and polishes the film itself. You need a degreaser to emulsify it first. See [petcoke hold cleaning](/blog/petcoke-hold-cleaning) for the full procedure.

## "Why is cargo trapped behind frames?"

Side frames and brackets create pockets: behind the frame web, on the upper face of the bracket, in the corner where the frame meets the hopper. A jet from the tank top hits the front and underside of these surfaces, not the back and top. Residue there is literally in the shadow of the jet.

**Fix:** get close. Use extension lances, a cherry picker or [rope access](/blog/cleaning-cargo-hold-upper-frames-without-scaffolding), and aim at the top faces and backs specifically. Inspect from above where possible.

## "Why does dust remain on upper structures?"

Three reasons. First, distance: a jet that has travelled 12–15 metres has lost most of its force. Second, angle: from below you're hitting undersides, but dust lies on top faces. Third, and the one people miss: dust that's knocked loose from higher up settles back onto ledges below.

**Fix:** clean top-down, from close range, and finish the upper structure before you do the final wash of the lower areas. Clean hatch covers and coamings first, so nothing falls back in.

## "Why is rust still visible after washing?"

Because washing removes loose rust, not rust itself. Tight, hard-adhering rust stays, and it's usually acceptable for grain clean. Loose scale may survive a wash at lower pressure.

**Fix:** test with a scraper. If it lifts, remove it mechanically or with higher-pressure washing. If it doesn't, it's probably acceptable. Check what the surveyor will accept. See [removing rust and loose paint](/blog/remove-rust-and-loose-paint-from-cargo-holds).

## "Why does the tank top remain dirty?"

Tank tops take the worst of everything: grab damage, bulldozer scratches, heavy residue. Residue sits in scratches and pitting where the coating is worn. Also, the tank top is where everything washed off the walls ends up, so if you wash the tank top before the walls are finished, you'll be washing it again.

**Fix:** wash the tank top last, after everything above it is finished. Treat stained areas with chemical and work them at close range with an HP lance or stiff brush. Squeegee to the bilge wells, don't just let water pool.

## "Why does cargo residue keep appearing after washing?"

It's coming from somewhere you haven't cleaned. Usual suspects:

- **Hatch covers and coamings**, dropping dust when covers move
- **Upper structure**, shedding residue onto the tank top as it dries and as the ship moves
- **Inside hollow structures**, such as hatch cover panels, pipe guards and ladders
- **Bilge wells**, backflowing residue onto the tank top
- **Adjacent holds**, via open hatches or a shared bilge system

**Fix:** clean in the right order (top first, outside first), isolate the bilge system between holds during cleaning, and check hatch cover undersides and the inside of pipe guards.

## "Why does the hold look clean but fail the inspection?"

Because you're looking at the tank top in daylight and the surveyor is looking at the tops of brackets with a torch. We've written about this separately: [why a hold that looks clean still fails inspection](/insights/why-a-clean-looking-hold-still-fails), and there's a full list of failure points in [why holds fail inspection](/blog/why-cargo-holds-fail-inspection).

## "How do I remove embedded cargo residue?"

"Embedded" usually means bonded into worn coating or rough steel. Work through these in order:

1. Chemical matched to the residue, with proper dwell time
2. Close-range HP washing, with a rotating nozzle
3. Mechanical agitation: stiff brush or scraper
4. Repeat locally
5. White-cloth test

If it still won't move and doesn't transfer, it's probably staining of the coating. Some surveyors accept that; some don't. For the long term, the fix is coating repair.

## "How do I clean difficult corners of a cargo hold?"

Corners where the tank top meets the hopper, the bulkhead stool and the side shell collect residue and water. Clean them by hand: scrape out residue, brush, then wash at close range. Check them again after the final rinse, since water and fines collect there.

## A quick checklist when the hold won't come clean

- [ ] Am I using a chemical matched to this residue?
- [ ] Am I giving it the full dwell time on a surface it can work on?
- [ ] Am I washing close enough?
- [ ] Am I cleaning top-down, with hatch covers done first?
- [ ] Is the residue coming back from somewhere I haven't cleaned?
- [ ] Is this mark residue (transfers to a cloth) or discolouration (doesn't)?
- [ ] Am I rinsing with fresh water at the end?

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does my hold still have black stains after pressure washing?
The stains are residue bonded to the coating, typically from coal or petcoke, which water alone can't remove. Use a suitable degreasing chemical with proper dwell time, then HP wash.

### Why doesn't high-pressure washing remove petcoke?
Green petcoke leaves an oily film that water can't dissolve. A degreasing chemical is needed to emulsify the film before HP washing removes it.

### Why does dust remain on the upper frames after washing?
Jets from the tank top lose force over distance and hit the undersides, while dust lies on top faces. Close-range cleaning from above is needed.

### Why does cargo residue keep reappearing after washing?
It's falling from somewhere not yet cleaned, such as hatch covers, upper structure, pipe guards or bilge wells, or coming from adjacent holds through the bilge system.

### How do I clean difficult corners of a cargo hold?
Scrape out residue by hand, brush, then wash at close range, and check again after the final rinse since water and fines collect there.
