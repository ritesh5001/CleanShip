Every hold wash produces dirty water, and every tonne of it has to go somewhere legal. For a long time the default was "over the side, once we're clear of land." Since the revised MARPOL Annex V came into force in 2013, that default has flipped: the starting point is that garbage, including cargo residues, goes ashore, with limited exceptions for discharge at sea.

Getting this wrong carries more than a fine. It's a detention risk, a reputational risk and, in some jurisdictions, a criminal one.

## What MARPOL Annex V says about cargo residues

MARPOL Annex V controls the discharge of garbage from ships, and garbage includes cargo residues: the remains of a cargo left on deck or in holds after loading or unloading, whether as sweepings or in wash water.

As P&I club guidance from Skuld and Britannia summarises it:

- **Residues of cargoes that are harmful to the marine environment (HME)** must not be discharged into the sea. They go to a port reception facility.
- **Residues of non-HME cargoes** (sweepings or in wash water) may be discharged at sea only if:
  - the ship is **en route**
  - it's at least **12 nautical miles from the nearest land** or ice shelf
  - it's **outside a special area**, unless all the conditions in regulation 6.2 are met
- **Cleaning agents and additives** in wash water may only be discharged if they're not harmful to the marine environment.

## What does HME mean, and who decides?

HME (harmful to the marine environment) is a classification of a solid bulk cargo based on criteria in MARPOL Annex III and Annex V. It's not up to the ship. The **shipper** is required to declare whether the cargo is HME, as part of the cargo information under the IMSBC Code. If the cargo declaration doesn't say, ask before loading. If you're not sure, treat it as HME until confirmed otherwise.

## Can hold wash water be discharged overboard?

Yes, if the residues and cleaning agents are not HME and the en route, 12-mile and special-area conditions are met. No, if any of those conditions fail. The practical checklist before opening the bilge valve:

- [ ] Cargo declared non-HME by the shipper
- [ ] Cleaning agents confirmed non-harmful (supplier declaration on board)
- [ ] Ship en route (not at anchor or drifting)
- [ ] At least 12 nm from nearest land
- [ ] Outside special areas, or regulation 6.2 conditions met
- [ ] Local or national rules don't impose stricter limits
- [ ] Entry ready for the Garbage Record Book

## MARPOL Annex V special areas

The special areas under Annex V are:

- Mediterranean Sea
- Baltic Sea
- Black Sea
- Red Sea
- "Gulfs" area
- North Sea
- Antarctic area (south of 60°S)
- Wider Caribbean region, including the Gulf of Mexico and the Caribbean Sea

Within special areas, discharge of cargo residues is much more restricted, and in the Antarctic area it is effectively prohibited. Check regulation 6.2 for the conditions that apply before relying on any exception.

## Can dirty wash water be discharged in port?

No. In port and at anchorage, you're within 12 nm of land and not en route, so discharge of residues into the sea isn't permitted under Annex V. Wash water generated in port must be retained on board for later lawful discharge or delivered to a reception facility. Many ports also have their own rules on washing alongside or at anchorage.

## Can cargo residue be discharged at anchorage?

Generally no, for the same reasons. Sweepings and wash water generated at anchorage must be retained or landed ashore.

## Cleaning agents: the part people forget

Hold-cleaning chemicals end up in the wash water, so their status matters. Skuld's guidance says a cleaning agent or additive is considered not harmful if:

- It's not a harmful substance under the criteria in MARPOL Annex III, and
- It contains no components known to be carcinogenic, mutagenic or reprotoxic

Get a statement from the supplier confirming this (often on the safety data sheet or a separate compliance statement) and keep it on board. If a cleaning agent is harmful, wash water containing it must be treated like HME residue, regardless of the cargo.

## Who collects contaminated wash water?

Port reception facilities, usually arranged through the ship's agent. That may be a shore tank, road tankers or a slop barge alongside. Things to plan for:

- **Notification:** most ports require advance notification of the waste to be delivered, often using the IMO standard advance notification form
- **Pumping arrangements:** the ship's bilge system may not be suitable for pumping residue-laden wash water ashore; portable pumps and hoses may be needed
- **Retention:** if wash water is retained on board, use a space approved for it
- **Receipts:** get a waste delivery receipt from the facility and keep it with the Garbage Record Book for at least two years

## What happens to petcoke and coal residue after cleaning?

Sweepings are usually bagged and landed ashore as garbage, or, for non-HME cargoes, discharged at sea under the conditions above. Petcoke and coal are often declared non-HME, but you need the shipper's declaration to rely on that. If they're HME, sweepings and wash water both go ashore.

## How should hold-cleaning chemicals be disposed of?

Unused chemicals go back to stores or ashore as hazardous waste, following the safety data sheet. Empty containers are garbage under Annex V. Wash water containing chemicals follows the rules above, depending on whether the agent is harmful.

## What records should the vessel keep?

Ships of 400 gross tonnage and above, and ships certified to carry 15 or more persons, must keep a Garbage Record Book. Following MEPC.277(70), it has two parts, with Part II for cargo residues. Each discharge or delivery should record:

- Date and time
- Position (latitude and longitude) or port name if delivered ashore
- Category of garbage
- Estimated amount, in cubic metres
- Start and stop positions for discharges at sea
- Signature of the officer in charge

Also keep the cargo's HME declaration, cleaning agent declarations, reception facility receipts and a hold cleaning log. If there's ever an investigation, these records are the ship's defence.

## Who pays for HME residue disposal?

Under standard voyage charter terms, residue removal is often for the owner's account. BIMCO's HME cargo residues disposal clause for voyage charters shifts the extra time and cost of storing and disposing of HME residues and wash water to the charterer. If you're fixing a cargo that's HME, or might be, get a suitable clause in place.

## Planning tips

- Find out the HME status of the cargo before you fix, not after discharge
- Plan washing for parts of the route where discharge is permitted
- Remove as much residue as possible during discharge, so less goes in the wash water
- Use non-harmful cleaning agents unless there's no alternative
- Book reception facilities early where needed

For practical planning of washing on passage, see [hold cleaning during the voyage](/blog/hold-cleaning-during-voyage).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can hold wash water be discharged overboard?
Only if the cargo residues and cleaning agents are not harmful to the marine environment, the ship is en route, at least 12 nautical miles from land, and outside special areas (unless regulation 6.2 conditions are met).

### What does MARPOL Annex V say about cargo residues?
HME cargo residues must not be discharged at sea and must go to a reception facility. Non-HME residues can be discharged at sea only en route, 12 nm or more from land and outside special areas, with discharges recorded in the Garbage Record Book.

### Can dirty hold wash water be discharged in port?
No. In port or at anchorage the ship is within 12 nm of land and not en route, so wash water must be retained on board or delivered to a reception facility.

### Who decides if a cargo is HME?
The shipper, who must declare whether the solid bulk cargo is harmful to the marine environment as part of the cargo information.

### What records are needed for cargo residue discharge?
Garbage Record Book Part II entries with date, time, position or port, category, estimated amount, start and stop positions for discharges at sea, and the officer's signature, plus reception facility receipts kept for at least two years.
