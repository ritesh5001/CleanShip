A hold-cleaning job is only as fast as its slowest piece of kit. One HP pump that keeps tripping, a chemical sprayer with a blocked nozzle or a hose that bursts at the bottom of the hold can cost more time than the residue itself. Here's what a properly equipped job looks like, and what each piece is for.

## The core kit

| Equipment | What it's for |
|---|---|
| Brooms, shovels, scrapers, buckets, bags | Dry removal of residue |
| Combi-gun / hold cleaning gun | High-volume washing from the tank top, often with chemical or air injection |
| High-pressure (HP) washer | Close-range cleaning of stubborn residue, stains and loose rust |
| Chemical sprayer or foam unit | Applying cleaning chemicals evenly |
| Fire main or dedicated hold wash pump | Water supply for combi-guns |
| Hoses, couplings and lances | Getting water and chemical to the surface |
| Bilge tools: scoops, buckets, portable pumps | Clearing bilge wells and sludge |
| Access equipment | Ladders, extension poles, cherry pickers, staging or rope access gear |
| Ventilation fans | Drying and gas-freeing |
| Gas detector | Atmosphere testing before and during entry |
| Lighting | Working and inspecting |
| PPE | Protection from water, chemicals, dust and noise |

## HP washer vs combi-gun (or "maxi-jet")

These are the two main washing tools, and they do different jobs.

**Combi-guns** (sometimes called hold cleaning guns or by brand names) run at relatively low pressure, typically fire main pressure of around 7–10 bar, but very high volume. They're mounted on a stand or held by an operator on the tank top, and throw a heavy jet a long way up the hold. Many can inject chemical or compressed air into the stream. They're good for:

- Rough washing of large areas
- Applying chemicals across the hold
- Reaching high areas (with reduced force)

**HP washers** run at high pressure (typically 200–500 bar) but much lower volume. They're used at close range with a lance, and they're what removes stubborn residue, staining and loose rust. They're good for:

- Final cleaning after chemical treatment
- Petcoke and coal films
- Cement and mineral deposits
- Spot cleaning problem areas

Most jobs use both: combi-gun for volume, HP washer for detail. We cover pressure selection in detail in a separate guide to high-pressure hold washing.

## What type of pump is used for hold cleaning?

- **For combi-guns:** the ship's fire pump or a dedicated hold-washing pump feeding the fire main or a separate line. Check that using the fire pump for cleaning doesn't compromise fire safety arrangements.
- **For HP washing:** a portable HP unit, electric or diesel driven. Electric units are cleaner and quieter but need sufficient power on deck; diesel units are self-contained but need exhaust management. Our own units include 500-bar, 30-litre-per-minute pumps.

## How many pumps are needed for a Panamax?

A Panamax or Kamsarmax usually has seven holds. If you're working two holds at a time, you'll want at least two HP units plus a spare, and one or two combi-gun lines. For a faster job working three or four holds at once, scale up accordingly. Always carry a spare HP unit, or at least spare hoses, nozzles and seals. Equipment failure is a common cause of delay.

## What chemical sprayer is best?

The best sprayer is the one that delivers the chemical evenly at the dilution required. Options include:

- **Air-operated diaphragm pumps** with a spray lance: robust, run on ship's air, handle a range of chemicals
- **Airless sprayers:** good coverage, often used for chemicals and limewash
- **Foam units:** foam clings to vertical surfaces and gives longer dwell time
- **Chemical injection on the combi-gun:** quick for large areas, less control over concentration

Check that seals and hoses are compatible with the chemical. Acids and strong alkalis attack some materials.

## What hose size should be used?

Use the hose rated for the pump's pressure and flow, and keep runs as short as practical. Typical:

- **HP hoses:** rated above the working pressure of the pump, commonly 3/8" or 1/2" bore for portable units
- **Combi-gun supply:** larger bore (often 2" or similar, matching fire hydrant connections) to deliver volume

Never use a hose with damaged covering or fittings at high pressure. A hose failure at 500 bar can cause serious injury.

## What type of nozzle should be used?

- **Fan (flat) nozzles** for general cleaning: the spray angle (e.g. 15°, 25°) trades coverage for impact
- **Zero-degree (pencil) nozzles** for stubborn spots, used carefully as they can damage coating
- **Rotating (turbo) nozzles** combine the impact of a pencil jet with the coverage of a fan. They're very effective on stubborn residue and many crews swear by them for petcoke and coal

## How do you remove sludge from bilges?

Bilge wells collect sludge: a mix of fine residue, water, rust and sometimes oil. To clear it:

1. Pump out free water
2. Scoop out sludge with buckets and shovels
3. Clean strainer plates and the bilge well
4. Wash and rinse
5. Test the bilge suction and non-return valves

For heavy sludge in bilges or tanks, specialist [demucking](/services/tank-cleaning/demucking) equipment and teams may be needed.

## What equipment is needed for demucking?

Demucking (removing mud and sediment, usually from ballast tanks) uses shovels, buckets, hoists, portable pumps, air-driven sludge pumps and sometimes vacuum units, plus enclosed space entry equipment: gas detectors, ventilation and rescue gear.

## Access equipment

Reaching the upper structure needs one of:

- Extension poles and lances (to a few metres)
- Cherry picker / boom lift
- Staging
- Rope access equipment (two-rope systems, harnesses, descenders, anchors), used by certified technicians

See [cleaning upper frames without scaffolding](/blog/cleaning-cargo-hold-upper-frames-without-scaffolding) for a comparison.

## PPE

At minimum, for each person:

- Helmet, safety boots, gloves
- Waterproof suit
- Eye and face protection (face shield for HP washing)
- Hearing protection (HP units are loud)
- Respiratory protection for dust and chemical mist
- Chemical-resistant gloves and suits for chemical work
- Personal gas detector where required by the enclosed space procedure

## Don't forget

- Spare parts: nozzles, seals, hose fittings
- Fuel and power for the pumps
- Fresh water for the final rinse
- Lighting for inspection
- A camera for before/after records

## Frequently asked questions

### What equipment is required for bulk carrier hold cleaning?
Brooms, shovels and scrapers; a combi-gun for high-volume washing; HP washers for close-range cleaning; chemical sprayers; pumps and hoses; bilge tools; access equipment; ventilation; gas detectors; lighting; and PPE.

### What's the difference between an HP washer and a combi-gun?
A combi-gun washes at low pressure and high volume over long distances, while an HP washer works at high pressure and low volume at close range for stubborn residue.

### How many pumps are needed to clean a Panamax?
For a seven-hold Panamax working two holds at a time, at least two HP units plus a spare and one or two combi-gun lines. More if several holds are worked at once.

### What is the best nozzle for hold cleaning?
Fan nozzles for general cleaning, and rotating (turbo) nozzles for stubborn residue such as petcoke and coal. Pencil jets are effective but can damage coating.

### Can robots replace manual hold cleaning?
Not yet. Some remote and robotic cleaning systems exist, but manual work is still needed for bilges, detail cleaning and inspection.
