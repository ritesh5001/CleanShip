"Who's cleaning the holds?" sounds like a simple question, and on board it usually is: the crew, unless someone's arranged otherwise. But behind it sit two harder questions that cause most hold-cleaning disputes: who has to make sure the holds are fit for the cargo, and who pays when they aren't?

This isn't legal advice. Charterparties vary, and the wording in yours decides the answer. But here's how responsibility usually breaks down.

## Who physically does the cleaning: crew or shore gang?

**Ship's crew** do most hold cleaning on most bulk carriers. It's part of normal shipboard work, and for straightforward changes (sweep, wash, rinse, dry) a competent crew with decent equipment can manage it.

The limits are:

- **Numbers.** A bulk carrier crew is typically around 20 people, and only some of them are free to work in the holds while still keeping watches and maintaining the ship.
- **Time.** Crew hours are capped by rest-hour rules under the MLC and STCW.
- **Equipment and access.** Crews may not have the HP equipment, chemicals or access gear for difficult jobs.

**Shore gangs** are contracted cleaning teams that work in port or at anchorage. **Riding crews** sail with the ship and clean on passage. Either is used when the job is too big, too dirty or too time-critical for the crew alone. See [riding crew hold cleaning](/blog/riding-crew-hold-cleaning) and our [shore gang service](/services/hold-cleaning/shore-gang).

Whoever does the physical work, the **Master** remains responsible for the ship's safety and for ensuring cargo spaces are fit to receive cargo, and the Chief Officer usually manages the cleaning.

## Who is responsible for having the holds ready?

Under most charterparties, the **owner** has a duty to present the ship with holds fit to receive the cargo. That's part of the owner's basic obligations about cargoworthiness, and charterparties typically spell it out in clauses requiring holds to be "clean, swept, washed down and dried", "grain clean", or "in every way fit to receive the cargo", to the satisfaction of the shipper's or charterer's surveyor.

The **charterer** usually sets the standard: they know the cargo, and the standard should be in the charterparty or voyage instructions.

## Who pays for hold cleaning?

That's where it depends on the type of charter.

### Voyage charter

The owner normally bears the cost of preparing holds for the cargo, since it's part of presenting a cargoworthy ship. Cleaning after discharge, removing residues, is also commonly for the owner's account. Some charterparties shift specific costs, such as HME residue disposal, to the charterer. BIMCO's HME cargo residues clause is an example, as Skuld's guidance highlights.

### Time charter

The charterer is directing the ship's employment, so cleaning between the charterer's cargoes is commonly for the charterer's account. Typical arrangements include:

- **Intermediate hold cleaning by crew,** with the charterer paying a lump sum per hold or per cleaning, under a clause that sets the rate and the standard
- **Charterer arranging and paying for shore cleaning** where the crew can't do it in time
- **A lump sum in lieu of hold cleaning on redelivery,** paid by the charterer to the owner, instead of the charterer redelivering the ship with clean holds

The owner usually remains responsible for the ship's ability to be cleaned (crew, equipment, condition of the holds), and some clauses limit the charterer's liability if holds fail through defects in the ship rather than cleaning.

### Is hold cleaning owner's or charterer's responsibility?

The honest answer: read the charterparty. Look for clauses dealing with:

- Hold condition and cleanliness on delivery and redelivery
- Intermediate hold cleaning: who does it, at what rate, to what standard
- Failure of inspection: off-hire, time lost, who pays for re-cleaning
- Residue disposal and HME cargoes
- Lump sums in lieu of cleaning

If the wording's unclear, agree it in writing before the work starts.

## Who decides the required cleanliness standard?

The charterer, normally, through the charterparty or voyage instructions, based on the cargo, the shipper's and receiver's requirements and local rules. The IMSBC Code also sets hold cleanliness requirements for listed cargoes. See [hold cleanliness standards](/blog/cargo-hold-cleanliness-standards).

## Can a charterer demand grain clean?

If the charterparty provides for it, yes, and it's very common. Charterers can't usually impose a higher standard than the charterparty requires, but vague wording ("suitable for the intended cargo") can amount to grain clean if that's what the intended cargo needs.

## What if the hold fails inspection?

Usually:

- If the holds fail because they weren't cleaned properly, and cleaning was the owner's responsibility, the ship may go **off hire** (time charter) or **time may not count as laytime** (voyage charter) until the holds pass
- If the holds fail because the charterer's surveyor imposed a standard beyond the charterparty, the owner may argue the time should count
- If the failure is because of the ship's condition (loose rust, broken coating), it's generally the owner's problem

Records decide these disputes: what was agreed, what was done, when, and what the surveyor found. Keep a cleaning log, photos and the surveyor's written report.

## Is it cheaper to use ship's crew or a shore gang?

The crew costs nothing extra in wages, but may cost days. A shore gang costs money but may save days. The comparison isn't crew wages vs gang price. It's gang price vs the value of the days saved, at the ship's hire rate. See [hold cleaning costs](/blog/bulk-carrier-hold-cleaning-cost) and [reducing hold cleaning delays](/blog/reduce-hold-cleaning-delays-and-demurrage).

## Frequently asked questions

### Who is responsible for hold cleaning, the crew or a shore gang?
The ship's crew does most routine hold cleaning. Shore gangs or riding crews are brought in when the job is too large, too dirty or too time-critical. The Master remains responsible for ensuring holds are fit for cargo.

### Is hold cleaning the owner's or the charterer's responsibility?
It depends on the charterparty. Under voyage charters the owner usually bears cleaning costs. Under time charters, intermediate cleaning is commonly for the charterer's account, often via a lump sum.

### Who pays if a hold fails inspection?
Usually whoever was responsible for the cleaning under the charterparty. If cleaning was the owner's duty, the ship may go off hire or time may not count until the holds pass.

### Can a charterer demand grain clean holds?
Yes, if the charterparty provides for it, which is very common. Vague wording may also amount to grain clean if the intended cargo requires it.

### What is a lump sum in lieu of hold cleaning?
A payment by the time charterer to the owner on redelivery, instead of the charterer redelivering the ship with clean holds. The owner then arranges the cleaning.
