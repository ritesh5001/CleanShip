A pre-inspection is the cheapest insurance in hold cleaning. An hour per hold with a torch and a scraper, and you find the problems while they're still yours to fix quietly, before they become a surveyor's report, an off-hire claim and a re-clean at anchor.

This is the checklist we'd run. Print it, take it into the hold, and don't skip sections because a hold "looks fine." That's how holds fail.

## Before you start

**Take with you:**

- A high-powered torch (hold it low across surfaces to show dust)
- A scraper or knife (to test rust and paint)
- A white cloth (to test stains)
- A camera or phone
- A copy of this checklist
- Gas detector, if required by the entry procedure

**Check first:**

- [ ] Hold atmosphere tested and entry permit in place
- [ ] Lighting on in the hold
- [ ] Required standard confirmed (grain clean, normal clean, etc.)
- [ ] Next cargo and any special requirements known

## Hatch covers and coamings

- [ ] Hatch cover undersides clean: no residue, no loose paint or rust
- [ ] Hatch girders and stiffeners clean on top faces
- [ ] Coaming tops, drain channels and drain valves clear
- [ ] Rubber seals intact and free of residue
- [ ] Compression bars clean and undamaged
- [ ] Cross-joints clean
- [ ] Weathertightness tested if required (hose or ultrasonic test)

## Upper structure

- [ ] Under-deck area clean
- [ ] Upper hopper tank sloping plates clean, with no dust lying on them
- [ ] Upper side frames clean, including the top faces of brackets
- [ ] Upper bulkhead stiffeners and corrugations clean
- [ ] No loose rust scale or flaking paint (test with scraper)
- [ ] White-cloth test at several points: no transfer

## Side shell and frames

- [ ] Side frames clean on all faces: front, back and top of brackets
- [ ] Frame webs and flanges clean
- [ ] No residue trapped behind frames
- [ ] No loose rust or paint

## Transverse bulkheads

- [ ] Clean top to bottom, including the upper and lower stools
- [ ] Corrugations clean in the recesses
- [ ] No loose rust or paint

## Ladders, platforms and fittings

- [ ] Ladder rungs and stringers clean
- [ ] Platforms and handrails clean
- [ ] Pipe guards clean on top and inside
- [ ] Sounding and air pipes clean
- [ ] Light fittings clean and working
- [ ] Fixed firefighting nozzles clear

## Lower hoppers and tank top

- [ ] Lower hopper plating clean
- [ ] Hopper-to-tank-top corners clean
- [ ] Tank top clean, no residue in scratches or pitting
- [ ] No standing water
- [ ] No loose scale

## Bilge wells

- [ ] Bilge wells clean and dry
- [ ] Strainer plates clean
- [ ] Bilge suctions tested and working
- [ ] Non-return valves working
- [ ] Covered as required (e.g. burlap for grain)
- [ ] No odour

## Moisture, odour and infestation

- [ ] Hold completely dry, including the underside of the hatch covers
- [ ] No condensation on bulkheads
- [ ] No odour of the previous cargo
- [ ] No insects, larvae or signs of rodents
- [ ] Chloride test done if required

## Records

- [ ] Photographs of each hold, including problem areas found and fixed
- [ ] Cleaning log complete: dates, methods, chemicals
- [ ] Garbage Record Book entries complete
- [ ] Condition reported to charterer and agent

## How to inspect properly

**Look from where the surveyor looks.** Stand on the tank top and look up with a torch. Then look down from the upper platforms and the coaming. You'll see different problems from each angle.

**Use the torch low and sideways.** A beam held at a shallow angle across a surface shows up dust and residue that a straight-on beam hides.

**Test, don't assume.** Scrape rust. Rub frames with a white cloth. Run your hand along the top face of a bracket.

**Check the places nobody cleaned.** Behind ladders, inside pipe guards, behind frames, in corners.

**Smell the hold.** Odour is a common failure that nobody checks.

## What should the Chief Officer check before calling the surveyor?

Everything on this list, in every hold. If time is short, prioritise the areas surveyors find dirty most often: top faces of frame brackets, hopper tops, hatch cover undersides, ladders and bilge wells. See [why holds fail inspection](/blog/why-cargo-holds-fail-inspection) for more.

## If you find problems

Fix them and recheck. Don't call the surveyor "and hope." If problems are on the upper structure and you can't reach them, consider a [rope access team](/services/hold-cleaning/rope-access).

## How to prepare a hold for a grain survey

Run this checklist with grain-clean standards in mind: no residue anywhere, no loose rust or paint, fully dry, no odour, no insects, bilges covered with burlap. The full guide is [how to prepare cargo holds for grain](/blog/how-to-prepare-cargo-holds-for-grain).

## Frequently asked questions

### What should a cargo hold inspection checklist include?
Hatch covers and coamings, upper structure, frames, bulkheads, ladders and fittings, lower hoppers, tank top, bilge wells, moisture, odour, infestation and records.

### How do you conduct a cargo hold pre-inspection?
Walk every hold with a powerful torch, scraper, white cloth and camera, check every area on the checklist from several angles, test rust and stains, smell the hold, fix anything you find and photograph the result.

### What should the Chief Officer check before calling the surveyor?
Every item on the checklist, with priority on the areas most often found dirty: top faces of frame brackets, hopper tops, hatch cover undersides, ladders and bilge wells.

### What areas do surveyors commonly find dirty?
Frame brackets, upper hopper tops, hatch cover undersides, ladder rungs and platforms, pipe guards and bilge wells.

### How do I prepare a hold for a grain survey?
Ensure no residue anywhere, no loose rust or paint, a completely dry hold, no odour or insects, and bilge wells cleaned, tested and covered as required.
