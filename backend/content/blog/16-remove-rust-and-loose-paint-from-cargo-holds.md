Every bulk carrier rusts. The question for hold cleaning isn't whether there's rust, but whether it's the kind that falls off. A grain surveyor will happily walk past a frame with solid, tight rust and then fail the hold for a patch of scale the size of a dinner plate that lifts under a knife.

## Loose rust vs hard rust: what surveyors accept

Skuld's guidance gives the practical test: loose rust scale breaks away when you strike it with a fist, or when you apply light pressure with a knife blade or scraper under its edge. Oxidation rust (the light, tight rusting that forms on bare steel) won't flake off under the same test.

For grain clean:

- **Hard-adhering rust** is normally acceptable, as long as it won't fall during loading or the voyage.
- **Loose scale and flaking paint** must be removed.
- **Some inspectors put limits on it.** The USDA standards, quoted by The Swedish Club, allow a single patch of loose scale or paint up to 10 sq ft, or several patches totalling up to 100 sq ft. Others, notably in Australia, may accept none.

So, can rust cause hold rejection? Loose rust can, and often does.

## How do you remove loose rust from cargo holds?

It depends on how much there is and where.

**Hand tools** (scrapers, chipping hammers, wire brushes) for small patches and spot work. Slow, but precise and needs no power.

**Power tools** (needle guns, rotary wire brushes, scaling machines) for larger areas. Faster, noisier, and they create dust that has to be cleaned up afterwards.

**High-pressure water washing** removes loose rust flakes and some lightly bonded scale, especially at higher pressures (around 500 bar). It's the quickest way to deal with widespread light scale on large surfaces.

**Ultra-high-pressure hydroblasting** (well above 1,000 bar) strips rust, scale and old coating back to steel. It's a surface preparation method for coating, not a routine cleaning tool, and is usually done by a specialist [hydroblasting team](/services/ndt-and-repair/hydroblasting).

## Can HP washing remove rust?

It removes loose rust well. That's why holds often look better after a heavy HP wash. It doesn't remove tight rust, and it isn't meant to. At very high pressures it starts to strip paint too, so be careful on sound coating.

## How do you remove scale from tank tops?

Tank tops get the hardest life in the hold: grabs, bulldozers and abrasive cargo. Scale builds up in layers, particularly in areas of broken coating. To remove it:

1. Sweep and wash first so you can see what you're dealing with
2. Lift loose scale with scrapers and chipping hammers, or a scaling machine on large areas
3. Follow with HP washing to clear the debris and lift any remaining loose flakes
4. Sweep out and remove the scale. It's residue that has to be disposed of like any other
5. Inspect, re-treat stubborn areas
6. Wash and rinse with fresh water

Should tank tops be painted after rust removal? Ideally, yes. Bare steel will rust again quickly, especially in the next wet cargo or sweat. Spot priming de-scaled areas extends the time before the next round. We cover painting in a separate guide on hold coating repair.

## Upper structure and hatch cover undersides

Loose paint and rust on the underside of hatch covers and the upper frames are dangerous for cargo: when the covers move or the ship works in a seaway, flakes fall into the cargo. They're also the hardest areas to reach. Options include cherry pickers, staging and [rope access](/blog/cleaning-cargo-hold-upper-frames-without-scaffolding). Rope access technicians can scrape and wire-brush the upper structure at close range, then spot-prime, all without staging.

## How do you clean rust before coating?

If the plan is to paint after de-rusting, surface preparation matters more than the paint:

- Remove all loose rust, scale and failed coating
- Wash off salts and contamination with fresh water. Salts under paint cause blistering
- Dry the surface completely
- Achieve the surface standard the paint manufacturer specifies (for example a power-tool or blast standard)
- Paint within the time window before flash rust forms

## How often should cargo holds be maintained?

Hold condition should be checked after every discharge and cleaning, as Britannia recommends, with defects noted and repaired promptly as part of the planned maintenance system. A regular cycle of spot de-scaling and touch-up painting is far cheaper than a big scaling job when the next grain fixture arrives with no time to do it.

## Fitting rust removal into a tight schedule

Rust removal is slow, and it's the stage most likely to blow a cleaning plan. Plan it:

- Inspect the holds as soon as they're empty and estimate the area of loose scale
- Start it early, in parallel with washing in other holds if possible
- Add people. Scaling is labour-intensive
- Use a [riding crew](/services/hold-cleaning/riding-crew) on passage if the port stay is short

## Frequently asked questions

### How do you remove loose rust from cargo holds?
Use hand tools for small patches, power tools for larger areas and high-pressure water washing for widespread light scale. Remove the debris, rinse with fresh water and spot-prime bare steel where possible.

### Can rust cause cargo hold rejection?
Loose rust scale can. Hard, tightly adhering rust is usually accepted, but scale that lifts under a scraper or flakes when struck will fail a grain inspection.

### Can high-pressure washing remove rust?
It removes loose rust flakes and some lightly bonded scale, especially at higher pressures, but not tight rust. Very high pressure can also strip sound paint.

### Should tank tops be painted after rust removal?
Ideally yes. Bare steel rusts quickly, so spot priming de-scaled areas protects the steel and reduces future scaling work.

### How do you tell loose rust from hard rust?
Loose scale breaks away when struck with a fist or when a knife or scraper is pushed under its edge. Hard oxidation rust won't flake off under the same test.
