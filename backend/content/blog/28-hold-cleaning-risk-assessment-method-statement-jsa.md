Paperwork doesn't clean holds. But a risk assessment that's actually thought through, a method statement that matches the job and a toolbox talk that the team actually listens to are what stop a hold-cleaning job from becoming an incident report. Britannia's guidance is clear that the Master should carry out a thorough risk assessment, with job-specific risk assessments, toolbox talks and proper PPE.

Below are working templates. Adapt them to your ship's safety management system, your cargo and your equipment. They're a starting point, not a substitute for thinking.

## Risk assessment vs method statement vs JSA vs toolbox talk

- **Risk assessment:** identifies hazards, who might be harmed, how likely and severe the harm is, and the controls that reduce the risk
- **Method statement:** describes how the job will be done, step by step, with the controls built in
- **Job safety analysis (JSA):** breaks the job into steps and identifies the hazards and controls for each step. Often used interchangeably with a task-level risk assessment
- **Toolbox talk:** a short briefing to the team before work starts, covering the job, hazards, controls and emergency arrangements

## Hold cleaning risk assessment template

| Hazard | Who could be harmed | Controls | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oxygen-deficient or toxic atmosphere | Anyone entering the hold | Gas test before entry at multiple levels; ventilation; entry permit; attendant at entrance; personal gas detectors; rescue plan and equipment | Low with controls |
| Fall from height (ladders, upper structure) | Workers at height | Three points of contact on ladders; harness and fall arrest; certified access equipment; rope access by certified technicians only; no work at height in heavy weather | Medium → low |
| High-pressure water injection | HP operators and others nearby | Trained operators only; dead-man trigger; never point at anyone; face shield, suit, boots, gloves; hose inspection; exclusion zone | Medium → low |
| Chemical burns and inhalation | Workers mixing and applying chemicals | Follow SDS; chemical PPE; ventilation; eyewash station; never mix products | Medium → low |
| Slips and trips | Everyone in the hold | Keep walkways clear; non-slip boots; good lighting; clear residue promptly | Medium → low |
| Falling objects | Workers below | Exclusion zones under work at height; tool lanyards; hard hats | Medium → low |
| Dust exposure | Workers sweeping | Respiratory protection; damping where permitted; ventilation | Medium → low |
| Noise | HP operators | Hearing protection | Low |
| Heat stress | Everyone in the hold | Work/rest rotation; drinking water; avoid hottest hours | Medium → low |
| Manual handling | Workers lifting residue and equipment | Bag size limits; mechanical lifting; team lifts | Medium → low |
| Hatch cover movement | Workers near covers | Hatch covers operated only by authorised crew; clear area; communication | Low |
| Pollution | Environment | MARPOL compliance; check HME status; record in GRB | Low with controls |

Rate likelihood and severity using your company's risk matrix.

## Hold cleaning method statement template

**1. Scope**
Holds: [numbers]. Previous cargo: [cargo]. Required standard: [standard]. Location: [port / anchorage / at sea].

**2. Team**
Supervisor: [name]. Team size: [number]. Rope access: [IRATA Level 3 supervisor, if applicable]. Responsible officer: [Chief Officer].

**3. Equipment**
HP pumps [number, pressure], combi-guns, chemical sprayers, hoses and lances, access equipment, gas detectors, ventilation fans, lighting, PPE.

**4. Chemicals**
[Product names], SDS on board, MARPOL status confirmed.

**5. Sequence**
1. Permits and gas test
2. Toolbox talk
3. Hatch covers and coamings
4. Dry sweep, top down; remove sweepings
5. Clear bilge wells
6. Seawater wash, top down
7. Chemical application per manufacturer
8. HP wash
9. Fresh water rinse
10. Bilge wells cleaned, tested, covered
11. Dry and ventilate
12. Pre-inspection and photographs

**6. Controls**
Reference the risk assessment.

**7. Emergency arrangements**
Rescue plan, communications, muster, first aid, medical evacuation.

**8. Records**
Cleaning log, GRB entries, photos, inspection report.

## Cargo hold cleaning JSA template

| Step | Hazards | Controls |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Prepare and gas test | Toxic or oxygen-deficient atmosphere | Test from outside at several levels; ventilate; permit |
| 2. Enter the hold | Falls on ladders | Three points of contact; tools hauled separately |
| 3. Sweep and remove residue | Dust; manual handling; slips | Respiratory protection; bag size limits; good housekeeping |
| 4. Clean bilge wells | Slips; manual handling; sludge contact | Gloves; team lifting; careful footing |
| 5. Wash with combi-gun | Slips; water pressure | Brace position; boots; clear area |
| 6. Apply chemicals | Burns; inhalation | Chemical PPE; ventilation; SDS; eyewash |
| 7. HP wash | Injection injury; noise | Trained operator; PPE; dead-man trigger; exclusion zone |
| 8. Work at height | Falls; falling objects | Harness; certified access; exclusion zone below |
| 9. Fresh water rinse | Slips | Boots; clear walkways |
| 10. Dry and inspect | Slips; poor visibility | Lighting; careful footing |
| 11. Exit and close permit | Falls; tools left behind | Account for all people and tools; close permit |

## Hold cleaning toolbox talk

Keep it short (10 minutes) and specific. Cover:

1. **The job:** which holds, what residue, what standard, how long
2. **Who's doing what:** teams, supervisor, attendant at the entrance
3. **The atmosphere:** gas test results, ventilation, re-test after breaks
4. **The big hazards today:**
   - HP water: never point the lance at anyone; report any injection injury immediately
   - Chemicals: which product, what PPE, where the eyewash is
   - Height: who's working above, exclusion zones below
   - Slips: wet and chemical-covered surfaces
5. **PPE:** what everyone must wear
6. **Communication:** radios, signals, who to call
7. **Emergency:** alarm, muster point, rescue plan, first aid
8. **Stop work:** anyone can stop the job if something is unsafe
9. **Questions**

Record attendance and sign it off.

## Making the paperwork useful

- **Make it job-specific.** A generic risk assessment for "hold cleaning" misses the cargo-specific hazards: methane after coal, sulphur dust, ammonia after fertiliser, phosphine after fumigated cargo.
- **Review it on the day.** Conditions change: weather, ventilation, team.
- **Involve the team.** The people doing the work spot hazards the paperwork misses.
- **Learn from it.** Near misses and incidents should feed back into the next assessment.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should a hold cleaning risk assessment include?
Hazards such as enclosed space atmospheres, falls from height, high-pressure water, chemicals, slips, falling objects, dust, noise, heat, manual handling, hatch cover movement and pollution, with who could be harmed, controls and residual risk.

### What is a hold cleaning method statement?
A step-by-step description of how the hold cleaning will be done, including scope, team, equipment, chemicals, sequence, controls, emergency arrangements and records.

### What is a JSA for cargo hold cleaning?
A job safety analysis that breaks hold cleaning into steps and identifies the hazards and controls for each step.

### What should a hold cleaning toolbox talk cover?
The job, who does what, atmosphere testing, the main hazards of the day, PPE, communication, emergency arrangements and everyone's authority to stop work.

### Who should carry out the hold cleaning risk assessment?
The Master and responsible officers, with input from the team doing the work and any contractor, under the ship's safety management system.
